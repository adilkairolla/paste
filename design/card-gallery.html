Card gallery

Every clipping kind, every selection state, and three competing treatments of the same card — drawn at 1 pt = 1 px so what you see is the size it ships at. The structure mirrors ClipCardView: a header, a flexible body that switches on ClipKind, and a footer.

Global

Anatomy

three fixed rows

The header and footer are fixed height; the body takes the rest. That's why a card can grow to any deck height without the type re-flowing — only the body clips.

Kinds

body switches on ClipKind

Eight cases, each with a body view of its own. Text and code differ only by font; link, file and colour earn bespoke layouts because their metadata is what you actually scan for.

States

selection, pin, category

Selection has to survive being seen at arm's length while you hold ⌘ — hence a filled background and a 2 pt accent ring, not one or the other.

Treatments

pick one

Same content, three ideas about where the metadata belongs. All three are a small diff to ClipCardView — the footer is one HStack.

A · shipped

Footer bar

Kind, category dot and the ⌘N shortcut sit on a tinted strip. Most legible, and the shortcut is always in the same place — but it costs 28 pt of body height on every card.

B · lightest

Floating badge

Kind moves into the header as a glyph; the shortcut floats over the content on a blurred pill. Gives images and code the full card. Risk: the badge can land on a busy part of a screenshot.

C · fastest scan

Accent spine

A 3 pt coloured edge encodes kind before you read anything. Colour alone isn't accessible, so the footer label stays — just without its background.

Row form

for rail & palette layouts

The side rail and centre palette need a list line rather than a card. Same data, one third the height — worth building as a second view rather than bending the card into it.
